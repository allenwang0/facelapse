facelapse

Turns a messy photo archive into a face-aging timelapse: it finds one person among group shots, duplicates and lookalike relatives, then aligns one front-facing photo per week or month.

The timelapse shown here is the pipeline's own output on 60 generated photos of drawn faces (2016–2024), with face detection stubbed. It shows every stage running; it measures nothing about real photos.[E4]

How it works

Smoothing the alignment sequence steadies the eyes#

Each photo's face is turned into an ArcFace embedding and kept only if it matches seed photos of the same person from the same era, by a margin over the next-best face; close calls go to a review page. Turned heads, strong expressions, tiny faces and near-duplicates are dropped, the best photo per period is chosen, and the eyes are aligned on a fixed canvas.

A per-photo alignment is exact but jumpy: landmark noise shifts where the eyes land from one frame to the next. facelapse smooths the sequence of alignments in canvas space before warping.[E3]

Rendering streams one frame at a time: on 1,000 synthetic 1600×1200 frames, the render step's peak resident memory was 143 MiB on Apple M5 Pro.[E2]

Limits. Faces in the demo are drawn and detection is stubbed, so nothing here measures identification or alignment accuracy on real photos; the face models (InsightFace buffalo_l) are licensed for non-commercial research only.
